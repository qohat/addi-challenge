One final artefact, for the reviewer rather than for us: docs/COMPLIANCE.md.

Go through docs/brief.pdf clause by clause and build a table: every requirement the brief states, where it lives in the code, and one line on the approach. Registry, judicial,
the parallelism constraint, the bureau with its durable cache, the graceful-failure-or-manual-review branch, the score rule, the CLI, no external database, external systems as
functions, simulated latency — and the process requirements too: spec-driven development with specs committed before implementation, the comprehensive README, and the AI
artifacts. Those three are half the grade and the easiest to leave implicit.

Cite files and types with paths, not prose descriptions, so a reviewer can jump straight there. One line of approach each. Where a decision has an ADR, link it instead of
re-explaining. Where we interpreted an ambiguity, link the assumption.

Every row that is only partially met has to say so. This is the part I care most about: a reviewer who finds an undeclared gap stops trusting the whole table, while one who sees
the gap declared trusts everything around it. So if a requirement is met by interpretation rather than literally, if coverage is thin somewhere, if something works but not the
way the brief probably imagined — write that in the row. Don't soften it into an approach description. I would rather hand them the weak spots myself than have them go looking.

Read README.md first and don't repeat it — that file says what the system is, how it works, what it cost and what's still missing. This one answers a different question: does it
do what was asked, and where do I look. If you find yourself restating a README section, link to it instead.
